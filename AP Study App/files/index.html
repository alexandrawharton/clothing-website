AP Biology

Cell biology, genetics, evolution, ecology, and biochemistry — core concepts for the AP Bio exam.

AP Exam
May 2026

Overview

All living organisms are made of cells. Prokaryotic cells (bacteria) lack a membrane-bound nucleus; eukaryotic cells (plants, animals, fungi, protists) have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

Key Organelles & Functions

  • Nucleus — stores DNA; transcription occurs here
  • Mitochondria — ATP production via cellular respiration
  • Ribosomes — protein synthesis (translation)
  • Endoplasmic Reticulum — protein/lipid processing (rough = ribosomes; smooth = lipids)
  • Golgi Apparatus — packages and ships proteins
  • Chloroplasts (plants) — photosynthesis

Key Terms

Prokaryote Eukaryote Organelle Endosymbiosis Cell Membrane Tonicity
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Know the fluid mosaic model of the cell membrane — phospholipid bilayer, integral proteins, cholesterol role in fluidity. Free-response questions frequently ask you to relate structure to function.

Central Dogma

DNA → mRNA (Transcription) → Protein (Translation). This is the foundational flow of genetic information in all living cells.

Replication Steps

  • Helicase unwinds the double helix at the origin of replication
  • Primase adds RNA primers to start synthesis
  • DNA Polymerase III adds nucleotides 5'→3'; leading strand is continuous, lagging strand is Okazaki fragments
  • Ligase seals the fragments

Key Terms

Helicase DNA Polymerase mRNA Codon Ribosome tRNA Anticodon
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Memorize the start codon (AUG = Met) and the three stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA). Know how a point mutation vs frameshift mutation affects protein structure.

Cellular Respiration (Aerobic)

  • Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate; net 2 ATP, 2 NADH
  • Pyruvate Oxidation (mitochondria): 2 pyruvate → 2 acetyl-CoA + 2 CO₂
  • Krebs Cycle: produces NADH, FADH₂, 2 ATP, CO₂
  • Electron Transport Chain + Oxidative Phosphorylation: ~32–34 ATP; O₂ is final electron acceptor

Photosynthesis

  • Light Reactions (thylakoid): captures light energy → ATP, NADPH, O₂ released
  • Calvin Cycle (stroma): uses ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂ into G3P (glucose precursor)
ATP NADH Glycolysis Krebs Cycle ETC Chlorophyll Calvin Cycle
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Know the inputs/outputs of each stage. A common FRQ asks: "What would happen to ATP production if…" — understand how blocking one stage affects the entire process.

Mendelian Genetics

Mendel's laws: Law of Segregation (alleles separate during gamete formation) and Law of Independent Assortment (genes on different chromosomes sort independently).

  • Monohybrid cross: Aa × Aa → 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa (3:1 phenotypic ratio)
  • Dihybrid cross: AaBb × AaBb → 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio
  • Incomplete dominance: blended phenotype
  • Codominance: both alleles expressed (e.g., blood type AB)
Allele Genotype Phenotype Punnett Square Epistasis Sex-Linked
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Practice chi-square analysis — you'll be expected to determine if results match expected Mendelian ratios and interpret p-values.

Darwin's Theory

Evolution by natural selection: variation exists in a population, traits are heritable, individuals with favorable traits survive and reproduce more, leading to allele frequency changes over generations.

Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

p + q = 1; p² + 2pq + q² = 1. A population is in H-W equilibrium when: no mutation, no migration, random mating, large population, no selection. Deviations indicate evolution is occurring.

Natural Selection Genetic Drift Gene Flow Speciation Hardy-Weinberg Phylogeny
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Hardy-Weinberg math appears on almost every AP Bio exam. Practice solving for p and q given phenotype frequencies and then calculating carrier frequencies.

Population Growth

  • Exponential growth: J-curve; dN/dt = rN (unlimited resources)
  • Logistic growth: S-curve; dN/dt = rN[(K-N)/K]; levels off at carrying capacity (K)
  • Density-dependent factors: predation, competition, disease
  • Density-independent factors: natural disasters, climate

Energy Flow

Only ~10% of energy transfers between trophic levels (10% rule). Producers (plants) → Primary consumers → Secondary consumers → Tertiary consumers.

Carrying Capacity Trophic Level Keystone Species Succession Biome
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Know the nitrogen, carbon, and water cycles in detail — be able to trace an atom through each cycle and name the organisms/processes involved.

AP Chemistry

Atomic structure, thermodynamics, kinetics, equilibrium, electrochemistry, and organic chemistry fundamentals.

AP Exam
May 2026

Quantum Model of the Atom

Electrons occupy orbitals described by quantum numbers: n (shell), l (subshell), mₗ (orbital), mₛ (spin). Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle, Hund's rule govern electron configuration.

  • Electronegativity increases up and right (F is highest)
  • Atomic radius increases down and left
  • Ionization energy increases up and right
Orbital Quantum Number Aufbau Principle Electron Config Periodic Trends
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Know how to write electron configurations including exceptions (Cr and Cu steal from 4s to half-fill or fill the 3d). Expect to explain periodic trend exceptions.

Bond Types

  • Ionic: electron transfer; metal + nonmetal; high melting point
  • Covalent: electron sharing; nonmetal + nonmetal; polar vs nonpolar
  • Metallic: delocalized electrons; explains conductivity, luster, malleability

VSEPR Theory

Electron pairs repel each other to minimize energy. Shape depends on bonding and lone pairs: linear (2), trigonal planar (3), tetrahedral (4), trigonal bipyramidal (5), octahedral (6).

VSEPR Hybridization Dipole Moment London Dispersion Hydrogen Bond
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Be able to draw Lewis structures, identify geometry, and determine if a molecule is polar. Polarity affects intermolecular forces, which affect boiling points — a common FRQ chain.

Key Equations

  • ΔG = ΔH – TΔS (spontaneous when ΔG < 0)
  • Hess's Law: ΔH_rxn = Σ ΔH_products – Σ ΔH_reactants
  • q = mcΔT (heat calculations)
  • ΔG° = –RT ln K (connects thermodynamics to equilibrium)

Spontaneity Table

ΔH –, ΔS +: always spontaneous. ΔH +, ΔS –: never spontaneous. Mixed signs: depends on temperature.

Enthalpy Entropy Gibbs Free Energy Hess's Law Calorimetry
🎯 AP Exam Tip: ΔG° = –RT ln K is one of the highest-yield equations. Practice converting between K, ΔG°, and reaction favorability direction.

Equilibrium Expressions

For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD: K = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ. Solids and pure liquids are excluded. Large K favors products; small K favors reactants.

Le Chatelier's Principle

  • Add reactant → shifts right (toward products)
  • Increase temperature in endothermic rxn → shifts right
  • Increase pressure → shifts toward fewer moles of gas
  • Catalyst → does NOT shift equilibrium; only speeds up attainment
K_eq Q (Reaction Quotient) ICE Table K_a / K_b K_sp
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Master the ICE table method for solving equilibrium problems. Compare Q to K to determine which direction the reaction proceeds.

Brønsted-Lowry Theory

Acids are proton (H⁺) donors; bases are proton acceptors. Conjugate acid-base pairs differ by one H⁺.

  • pH = –log[H⁺]; pOH = –log[OH⁻]; pH + pOH = 14 at 25°C
  • Strong acids/bases fully dissociate (e.g., HCl, NaOH)
  • Weak acids: use K_a and ICE table to find pH
  • Henderson-Hasselbalch: pH = pK_a + log([A⁻]/[HA])
pH / pOH K_a / K_b Buffer Titration Henderson-Hasselbalch
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Titration curves appear every year. Know the equivalence point, buffer region (midpoint = pKa), and how to choose an indicator.

Redox Basics

  • OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons), Reduction Is Gain
  • Oxidizing agent gets reduced; reducing agent gets oxidized
  • Balance redox reactions using half-reaction method

Electrochemical Cells

  • Galvanic (voltaic) cell: spontaneous; generates electricity (ΔG < 0, E°cell > 0)
  • Electrolytic cell: nonspontaneous; requires electrical energy input
  • Nernst Equation: E = E° – (RT/nF)lnQ
Oxidation State Half-Reaction E°cell Nernst Equation Faraday's Constant
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Know how to calculate E°cell from standard reduction potentials (E°cathode – E°anode) and relate it to ΔG° = –nFE°.

AP Physics (1 & C)

Mechanics, energy, waves, electricity, magnetism, and modern physics — conceptual and mathematical mastery.

AP Exam
May 2026

Kinematic Equations (constant acceleration)

  • v = v₀ + at
  • x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²
  • v² = v₀² + 2aΔx

Newton's Laws

  • 1st: An object at rest/motion stays so unless acted on by net force
  • 2nd: F_net = ma (vector equation!)
  • 3rd: Every action has equal and opposite reaction
Displacement Velocity Acceleration Free Body Diagram Normal Force Friction
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Always draw a free-body diagram before writing Newton's 2nd Law equations. Define a positive direction and be consistent. Projectile problems always have a_x = 0, a_y = –g.

Key Equations

  • W = Fd cosθ (work = force × displacement × cos of angle)
  • KE = ½mv²; PE_gravity = mgh; PE_spring = ½kx²
  • Work-Energy Theorem: W_net = ΔKE
  • Conservation of Energy: E_total = KE + PE = constant (no friction)
  • Power: P = W/t = Fv
Work Kinetic Energy Potential Energy Conservation Power Hooke's Law
🎯 AP Exam Tip: For energy problems, identify initial and final states and list all energy types present. If friction is present, include W_friction = –f_k × d as a negative work term.

Core Concepts

  • Momentum: p = mv (vector quantity!)
  • Impulse: J = FΔt = Δp
  • Conservation of Momentum: p_before = p_after (closed system)
  • Elastic collision: KE conserved; Inelastic: KE lost; Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together

Center of Mass

x_cm = Σ(mᵢxᵢ) / Σmᵢ. The CM of a system moves as if all external forces act on it.

Momentum Impulse Elastic Inelastic Center of Mass
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Momentum is always conserved in collisions (no external forces). Energy is only conserved in elastic collisions. Distinguish these carefully in FRQs.

Electrostatics

  • Coulomb's Law: F = kq₁q₂/r²
  • Electric field: E = F/q = kQ/r²; points away from + charges
  • Electric potential: V = kQ/r; PE = qV
  • Gauss's Law: Φ_E = Q_enc/ε₀

Circuits

  • Ohm's Law: V = IR
  • Series: R_total = ΣRᵢ; same current
  • Parallel: 1/R_total = Σ(1/Rᵢ); same voltage
  • Power: P = IV = I²R = V²/R
Coulomb's Law Electric Field Ohm's Law Kirchhoff's Laws Capacitor
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Use Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (sum of voltages around a loop = 0) and Kirchhoff's Current Law (sum of currents into a node = 0) for complex circuits.

Magnetic Force

  • Force on moving charge: F = qv × B (use right-hand rule)
  • Force on current-carrying wire: F = IL × B
  • Circular motion in B field: qvB = mv²/r → r = mv/qB

Faraday's Law

EMF = –ΔΦ/Δt. A changing magnetic flux induces an EMF. Lenz's Law: induced current opposes the change in flux.

Magnetic Flux Faraday's Law Lenz's Law Right-Hand Rule Inductance
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Practice the right-hand rule for force on a charge, force on a wire, and direction of induced current. Getting the direction right earns points even if the magnitude calculation has errors.

Wave Properties

  • v = fλ; period T = 1/f
  • Transverse vs longitudinal waves
  • Superposition, interference (constructive/destructive), diffraction
  • Standing waves: nodes and antinodes; harmonics

Modern Physics

  • Photoelectric effect: E = hf; work function W; KE_max = hf – W
  • de Broglie wavelength: λ = h/mv
  • Mass-energy equivalence: E = mc²
Wave Speed Interference Doppler Effect Photon de Broglie
🎯 AP Exam Tip: The photoelectric effect is a classic AP question. Know that increasing intensity increases the number of electrons ejected (not their energy); only increasing frequency increases KE_max.

AP Psychology

History & approaches, biological bases, sensation, learning, memory, cognition, development, motivation, and social psychology.

AP Exam
May 2026

The Neuron

  • Dendrites receive signals; axon transmits; terminal buttons release neurotransmitters
  • Action potential: all-or-nothing firing; resting potential –70 mV
  • Synapse: neurotransmitters cross synaptic cleft and bind to receptors
  • Reuptake: neurotransmitter is reabsorbed by the sending neuron

Key Neurotransmitters

  • Dopamine — reward, movement (low: Parkinson's; excess: schizophrenia)
  • Serotonin — mood, sleep, appetite (low: depression)
  • GABA — main inhibitory NT (low: anxiety)
  • Acetylcholine — muscle control, memory (low: Alzheimer's)
  • Norepinephrine — fight-or-flight, alertness
Neuron Action Potential Synapse Neurotransmitter Reuptake Agonist/Antagonist
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Know which neurotransmitter is associated with each disorder and what happens when there's too much or too little. This is heavily tested in FRQs.

Key Concepts

  • Sensation: detecting stimulus energy (bottom-up processing)
  • Perception: interpreting sensory input (top-down processing)
  • Absolute threshold: minimum stimulus detectable 50% of the time
  • Difference threshold (JND): smallest detectable difference between stimuli
  • Signal detection theory: response depends on sensitivity AND bias

Vision

Rods (low light, periphery) and cones (color, detail) in the retina. Trichromatic theory (3 cone types) + opponent-process theory explain color vision.

Transduction Absolute Threshold Weber's Law Gestalt Principles Depth Perception
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Know Weber's Law (ΔI/I = k; the JND is a constant proportion of the original stimulus) and how it applies to real-world examples.

Classical Conditioning (Pavlov)

  • US (food) → UR (salivation)
  • CS (bell) paired with US → CR (salivation to bell)
  • Extinction: CS presented without US → CR weakens
  • Spontaneous recovery, generalization, discrimination

Operant Conditioning (Skinner)

  • Positive reinforcement: add pleasant stimulus → behavior increases
  • Negative reinforcement: remove unpleasant stimulus → behavior increases
  • Positive punishment: add unpleasant stimulus → behavior decreases
  • Negative punishment: remove pleasant stimulus → behavior decreases
CS / US / CR / UR Reinforcement Punishment Schedules Shaping Bandura
🎯 AP Exam Tip: The most common FRQ error: confusing negative reinforcement with punishment. Negative reinforcement INCREASES behavior (by removing something bad). Always check: does the behavior go up or down?

Atkinson-Shiffrin Memory Model

  • Sensory memory → Short-term (working) memory → Long-term memory
  • Encoding: getting info in (elaborative rehearsal > maintenance)
  • Storage: retaining info
  • Retrieval: getting info out (recall vs recognition)

Forgetting

  • Proactive interference: old memories interfere with new
  • Retroactive interference: new memories interfere with old
  • Repression, decay, encoding failure
Explicit Memory Implicit Memory Working Memory Interference Chunking Mnemonics
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Know the difference between explicit (declarative: episodic + semantic) and implicit (procedural, conditioned) memory. The hippocampus is essential for forming new explicit memories.

Piaget's Cognitive Stages

  • Sensorimotor (0–2): object permanence, sensory exploration
  • Preoperational (2–7): symbolic thinking; egocentrism; lack of conservation
  • Concrete Operational (7–11): logical thinking about concrete events; conservation
  • Formal Operational (12+): abstract, hypothetical reasoning

Erikson's 8 Psychosocial Stages

Each stage presents a conflict (e.g., Trust vs. Mistrust in infancy; Identity vs. Role Confusion in adolescence; Integrity vs. Despair in late adulthood).

Object Permanence Conservation Egocentrism Attachment Kohlberg Stages
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Be ready to apply a theorist to a scenario. "A child believes the world revolves around them" → Piaget's egocentrism (preoperational). "A teen asks 'Who am I?'" → Erikson's Identity vs. Role Confusion.

Key Phenomena

  • Conformity (Asch): people conform to group norms even when wrong
  • Obedience (Milgram): 65% continued to 450V when ordered by authority
  • Bystander effect: less likely to help with more people present (diffusion of responsibility)
  • Fundamental Attribution Error: overattribute others' behavior to character; underattribute to situation

Attitudes & Behavior

  • Cognitive dissonance: discomfort from conflicting beliefs → change attitude/behavior
  • Mere exposure effect: more exposure → more liking
  • Self-serving bias: attribute success to self, failure to situation
Attribution Conformity Obedience Groupthink Prejudice Stereotype
🎯 AP Exam Tip: Social psychology concepts appear in almost every FRQ. Know the classic studies (Milgram, Asch, Zimbardo) — their findings, criticisms, and ethical issues.
